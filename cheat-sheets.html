Module 1: Artificial intelligence

Key ideas

  • AI is software that does tasks that usually need a human mind.
  • The parts: AI > machine learning > deep learning > generative AI. Each is a part of the larger area.
  • In ML, the computer finds the rules in data. A person does not write the rules.
  • Training is a loop: guess, compare, adjust, repeat.
  • A chat AI predicts the next token again and again.

Remember

  • All AI today is narrow AI. AGI does not exist at this time.
  • A neural network has layers of neurons. Weights connect the neurons.
  • AI became strong when data, fast chips, and better methods came together.
  • Dates: 1950 Turing test, 1956 the name "AI", 2012 deep learning, 2017 transformer, 2022 public chat AI.

Watch out

AI can make errors and can sound sure when it is wrong (hallucination). It can learn bias from its data. Always do a check of important information.

Module 2: Mathematics for machine learning

Statistics and probability

  • A model is a function with parameters. Training finds good values for them.
  • Mean, median, and standard deviation describe data. The median is not changed much by outliers.
  • Many measurements have a normal distribution: 68% of values are less than 1 standard deviation from the mean, 95% less than 2.
  • Correlation shows that two values change together. It does not show cause.
  • Bayes' rule changes a probability with new information. Rare events give many false alarms.

Vectors and calculus

  • A vector is a list of numbers. A matrix is a table of numbers.
  • A neural layer multiplies a matrix by a vector, and adds a bias.
  • Softmax changes scores into probabilities. Cross-entropy measures the loss.
  • The derivative is the slope. Gradient descent moves opposite to the slope.
  • The chain rule makes backpropagation possible.

Watch out

Standardize features that have very different sizes. Otherwise the largest feature hides the others. For all formulas, refer to Sheet 12, Formula sheet.

Module 3: Machine learning

The workflow

  1. Define the problem.
  2. Collect data.
  3. Prepare the data, and divide it: training, validation, test.
  4. Select a type of model.
  5. Train the model.
  6. Evaluate it with data that it did not see.
  7. Put it into use.
  8. Monitor its results.

Types of learning

  • Supervised: examples with labels. Classification (a category) or regression (a number).
  • Unsupervised: no labels. Find groups (clustering).
  • Reinforcement: actions and rewards.

Fit

  • Underfitting: errors on training and new data. Model too simple.
  • Overfitting: good on training, bad on new data. Use more data, a simpler model, or stop early.

Measure a classifier

  • Accuracy: correct predictions ÷ all predictions.
  • Precision: TP ÷ (TP + FP). Important when a false alarm is expensive.
  • Recall: TP ÷ (TP + FN). Important when a missed item is dangerous.
  • High accuracy is not always good when one category is rare.

Common methods

  • Linear regression: a straight line. Prices.
  • Logistic regression: a probability for a category.
  • Decision tree and random forest: yes/no questions, many trees vote.
  • k-nearest neighbors: the most similar examples.
  • k-means: k groups around k centers.

Module 4: Deep learning

Key ideas

  • Deep learning uses neural networks with many layers. The network finds the features in the raw data.
  • A neuron multiplies its inputs by weights, adds a bias, and applies an activation function.
  • Backpropagation finds the gradient for each weight. Gradient descent adjusts the weights.
  • Embeddings change items into lists of numbers. Similar items have similar embeddings.
  • Transformers use attention to connect each token to the other tokens.

Types of network

  • Feedforward: numbers in a table.
  • CNN: images. Filters find patterns.
  • RNN: sequences, one item at a time. Mostly replaced by transformers.
  • Transformer: text, and now also images and sound.

In practice

Training needs GPUs. To prevent overfitting, use dropout, data augmentation, and early stopping. With little data, use transfer learning: start from a network that is already trained, and train it a little more on your data.

Module 5: Generative AI

How it is made and how it answers

  • Generative AI makes new text, images, sound, video, and code.
  • Three stages: pretraining, fine-tuning, human feedback.
  • The context window limits how much text the model can use at one time.
  • An image generator starts from noise and removes it step by step.
  • RAG lets a model use your documents and show its sources.

Temperature

  • Low (0 to 0.3): almost the same answer each time. Facts, code, data.
  • Medium (0.5 to 0.8): usual chat and writing.
  • High (1.0 or more): varied, more errors. Ideas and stories.

A good prompt

  • The task (always necessary).
  • The context and the audience.
  • The format of the answer.
  • One to three examples (few-shot prompting).
  • For logic, ask for the steps. Divide a large task.

Watch out

  • AI-made content can be false. Always do a check before you use it.
  • Do not put private data into a public AI tool.
  • Text in web pages and documents can contain hidden instructions (prompt injection).

Module 6: Responsible AI

Principles

  • Fairness: works equally well for all groups.
  • Transparency: people know that AI is used.
  • Privacy: personal data only with permission.
  • Safety: no physical or financial harm.
  • Accountability: a person is responsible, not the AI.
  • Human oversight: a person can check, change, or stop decisions.

Bias and law

  • Bias comes from the data, the labels, the design, or the use.
  • Measure the results for each group separately.
  • India: DPDP Act (2023). EU: GDPR (2018) and the EU AI Act (2024), with four levels of risk.
  • Data minimization: collect only the data that you need.

Do not use AI when

A simple rule works. There is no good data. Nobody can check the errors. The law requires an explanation that you cannot give. The task needs human care. The cost is larger than the benefit. "Do not use AI" is a correct result of a careful plan.

Module 7: AI in practice

Before training

  • Missing values: remove, fill with the median (imputation), or add a "missing" feature.
  • Categories: one-hot encoding, not 1, 2, 3.
  • Data leakage: divide the data first. Use only information that is available at the time of the prediction.
  • Compare each model with a simple baseline.
  • Use cross-validation to select hyperparameters. Use the test set only one time.

After training

  • Ways of deployment: batch, online, on the device.
  • Release: version number, shadow mode, A/B test, then all users. Keep the old version.
  • Data drift: the inputs change. Concept drift: the correct answers change.
  • Monitor real results, set alert limits, and train again with new data.

LLM applications and agents

Test each new prompt or model with a fixed evaluation set. Use guardrails before and after the model. Calculate the cost: tokens × price. Use an agent only when the steps must change. Give it limited tools, a maximum number of steps, a record of each step, and human approval for important actions.

Module 8: DevOps for AI

Key ideas

  • DevOps: one team builds and operates, with small changes, automation, and fast feedback.
  • MLOps adds versions of data and models, model tests, and training again.
  • LLMOps adds prompt versions, evaluation sets in the pipeline, and cost monitoring.
  • Keep a version of code, data, models, prompts, and libraries.

Build and release

  • Serve the model as an API. Check each input. Send the model version with each answer.
  • Pin library versions. Put the service in a container.
  • Environments: development, staging, production.
  • Pipeline: tests, build, staging, canary, production. Quality gates stop bad models.

Operate

  • Observability: logs, metrics, traces, dashboards, alerts.
  • SLO example: 99.9% availability = 43.2 minutes without service in 30 days.
  • Use the p95 latency, not the mean.
  • Incident: confirm, roll back, find the cause, repair, write a postmortem.

Watch out

  • Never put secrets in code or Git. If one gets in, change it immediately.
  • Give each service the least access.
  • Set a cost budget and an alert in the cloud.
  • Keep personal data out of the logs.

Module 9: LLM engineering

Call the model

  • Keep the key in ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, never in code.
  • Set model, system, max_tokens, messages.
  • Check stop_reason. Read usage for the tokens.
  • Structured output: a Pydantic class and messages.parse.

RAG

  1. Divide documents into chunks (200 to 500 words, small overlap).
  2. Make a vector for each chunk with an embedding model.
  3. For each question, find the k most similar chunks.
  4. Prompt: only the sources, cite [1], say "I do not know", sources are data.

Tools and evaluation

  • The model asks for a tool. Your program runs it and sends the result back.
  • Limit the steps. Check the inputs. A person approves important actions.
  • Test the retrieval with recall at k. Test the answers with a judge and a reference.
  • Check a sample of the judge's grades.

Cost and choices

  • Cost = input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price.
  • Prompt caching: the fixed text first, the question last.
  • Batches cost half. A smaller model for simple tasks.
  • Order: a better prompt, then RAG, then fine-tuning (LoRA).

Module 10: ML system design

Seven steps

  1. Requirements: users, scale, latency, cost of errors.
  2. Metrics: business, model, guardrail.
  3. Data: sources, labels, delay, privacy, leakage.
  4. Model: baseline first.
  5. Serving: batch, online, or device; latency budget.
  6. Monitoring: drift, new labels, training again.
  7. Risks and trade-offs.

Estimates

  • Requests each second = requests each day ÷ 86,400. Peak = 3 to 5 × mean.
  • Divide the latency budget between the network, the features, the model, and the rules.
  • Storage = size of a record × records each day.
  • Cost each month = cost of one request × requests each month.

Thresholds and labels

  • Total cost = missed × cost of a miss + false alarms × cost of a false alarm.
  • A miss is expensive: lower the threshold.
  • Labels can arrive weeks later. Divide data by date.

Lessons from failures

  • Amazon hiring tool: past decisions contain bias.
  • Zillow Offers: drift and large bets on predictions.
  • Google Flu Trends: correlations that change.

Formula sheet

All formulas of Module 2 and the measurements of Module 3. Σ means "add all the values".

Statistics

Mean
mean = Σx ÷ n
Median
the middle value after you sort the valuesWith an even number of values: the mean of the two middle values.
Standard deviation
√( Σ(x − mean)² ÷ n )
z-score
z = (x − mean) ÷ standard deviationUsed for standardization.
Correlation r
from −1 to +1+1: up together. 0: no straight-line relation. −1: one up, one down.

Probability

Probability
P = favorable results ÷ all resultsAlways from 0 to 1.
Not A
P(not A) = 1 − P(A)
Bayes' rule
P(A | B) = P(B | A) × P(A) ÷ P(B)
Softmax
pi = esi ÷ Σ esjChanges scores into probabilities that add up to 1.
Cross-entropy
loss = −log(p of the correct answer)Small when the model gives the correct answer a high probability.

Vectors and matrices

Dot product
a · b = a1b1 + a2b2 + …
Length
|a| = √(a · a)
Cosine similarity
(a · b) ÷ (|a| × |b|)1: same direction. 0: not related.
Neural layer
outputs = activation( W · x + b )

Calculus and training

Slope
change in y ÷ change in x
Example derivative
f(x) = x² → f′(x) = 2x
Gradient descent
new weight = weight − learning rate × slope
Chain rule
slope of y for x = (slope of y for u) × (slope of u for x)

Measure a model

Accuracy
(TP + TN) ÷ all predictions
Precision
TP ÷ (TP + FP)
Recall
TP ÷ (TP + FN)
Mean absolute error
Σ|real − predicted| ÷ nFor regression and forecasts. Refer to the forecasting topic.

TP: true positive. TN: true negative. FP: false positive (false alarm). FN: false negative (missed item).
